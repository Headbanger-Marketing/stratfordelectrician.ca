Before work begins

Keep a record of what is there and what changes.

An older house altered over the years may hold wiring of one age and method behind one wall and a different kind behind the next, and nobody may have noted who changed what. Records can have gaps in a newer house too.

Whatever the home, start a file: photographs of the panel door and its labels, any drawings, invoices and ESA paperwork, and a note of which rooms were changed. The contractor can compare the records with what is found.

What records should I gather before asking for electrical work?

Gather panel door labels, any drawings, invoices from earlier work, ESA paperwork and inspection or seller reports. Photograph labels from the outside and note what is missing, because a gap in the records is useful information for the contractor.

Can I rely on what the panel door says about each circuit?

Not reliably. Labels can be old, partial or written by someone who has since moved on. Ask the contractor to confirm each circuit during the work and to leave a clear updated directory for the next owner.

Why do two proposals for similar work differ when records are thin?

Each contractor fills gaps in the records with assumptions about the existing wiring, and those assumptions can differ. Ask each one to state what was assumed and what would change the scope if it proves wrong.

After the job ends, where should the electrical records be filed?

Keep a copy yourself and ask the contractor for the written scope, the notification details and any drawings or label updates. Store them together where the next person who owns the home can find them.
