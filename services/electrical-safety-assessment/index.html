A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Stratford property

Write down every alteration you know of or suspect, such as a finished room, a moved appliance or an added circuit, and gather any invoices, drawings or ESA paperwork that mention it.

Ask the contractor how undocumented work will be handled in the report, whether anything concealed will be marked as not reviewed and who will receive a copy of the written result.

What should the assessment establish?

Every area and piece of equipment reviewed appears with the method used on it, even where earlier work left no paperwork to go on. A separate part names what could not be reached, such as concealed wiring, so the limits are visible. For any item that looks altered, a factual description comes with the further steps suggested, without any guess at who made the change. Any follow-up notification that would be needed is stated too. The record is specific enough to pass to a later Stratford owner without overstating what was checked.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can an assessment tell me who did earlier electrical work in my home?

Not usually. It describes what is visible and reachable now, not who installed it or when. Records such as invoices, labels and earlier ESA paperwork are what can point to that, so gather them first.

What written result should I ask for after an assessment of past work?

Ask for a summary that lists the areas reviewed, the areas not reached, anything that looks altered and the next steps suggested. A clear summary can then be filed with your other electrical records.
