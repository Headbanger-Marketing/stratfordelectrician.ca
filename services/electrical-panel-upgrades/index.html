Panel and service upgrades in Stratford.

Planning a sub-panel for a garage, basement or addition in a Stratford home begins with the panel it would hang from. The thinner the paperwork on that panel, the more the contractor must discover on site.

A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Stratford property

Photograph the existing panel door with its directory, sketch how far the garage, basement or addition lies from it and list any earlier changes you remember.

Ask the contractor how the current panel was identified, what the new feed would join and whether each circuit moved across will be listed in writing.

What should the assessment establish?

Agreeing to a sub-panel for a garage, basement or addition means having on record what the present panel and its incoming feed already carry. Their labels and drawings count as leads, not proof. The report states whether the loads planned for the new space sit comfortably beside what is already connected, and it names where the sub-panel goes, how it is fed and who labels each circuit. Whether the utility is expected to take part is noted as well. At completion, a revised directory and the notification details let a later owner of the Stratford house see what changed and why.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can a sub-panel go in before the main panel records are sorted out?

Only the contractor can say, after seeing the existing equipment. Bringing labels, drawings and earlier paperwork to that visit gives them something to compare against, and any gaps can be noted in the scope.

What should a sub-panel directory show once the work is done?

Every circuit should have a plain description of what it feeds and where, while the first panel's directory should name the breaker that supplies the sub-panel. Photograph both so the record stays with the home.

Useful project details

  • Reason for the upgrade and known concerns
  • Existing major loads and proposed additions
  • Panel information visible without removing covers
  • Equipment location and access restrictions
  • Preferred timing and equipment that must remain operating

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
